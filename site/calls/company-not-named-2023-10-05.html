Owner off plan, unsure his sales manager manages
Not named on the call — Joe Beck's technical products company (four full-time salespeople; his partner is a double-E)
Rob LimeAdvancedProspect

Joe and an engineer partner built a technical products company that is running roughly ten to fifteen percent behind the growth they project each year, and his sales manager is simply the first salesperson he ever hired. Rob ran his three-frustration positioning statement, pulled the symptom out (quotes and follow-ups going out weeks late, pipeline tracked in homemade Teams buckets), tested whether it actually costs them business, and then asked whether the manager isn't held accountable, doesn't know, or refuses. Joe took the blame himself, Rob affirmed it, and they booked an hour for the following Tuesday.

Booked a one-hour Zoom for Tuesday the 10th at 2:00 Central, continuing the same line of questioning and opening up what Lushin does; Joe chose to keep his partner out of it.

Coaching notes❯

The three-frustration positioning statement at 4:20 is clean and reusable: not enough opportunities, can't close predictably or at margin, managers can't develop the team. It gave Joe a menu and he chose from it immediately.

Joe said ten percent off plan at 9:39. Rob restated it as fifteen percent at 10:16 and again at 10:54 and Joe never corrected him. The deal survived, but watch how easily a mis-restated number sticks — it could just as easily have cost credibility.

The impact question got a shrug — 'I don't know a hundred percent sure, but I would imagine' — and Rob moved on. The call ends with no quantified pain and no dollars attached to late quotes, which is the thing the second meeting has to fix.

Letting Joe blame himself and then affirming it is what earned the hour. Compare the temperature before and after 12:54.

No budget, no criteria, no competition — deliberately held back, and he contracted for the next conversation out loud at 13:33 so the deferral was explicit rather than an oversight.
