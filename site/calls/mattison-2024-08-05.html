Referral call disqualified in fourteen minutes
transcribed as 'Madison'; an association management company serving nonprofits
Shad TidlerNo fitProspect

Andy Chapman connected Shad to Scott, who co-owns an association management company that staffs and runs about fifteen larger nonprofits and has been around since the eighties. Scott took the call out of respect for Andy, not out of need: the firm grows through client growth, new programs and acquisitions, passes on 90% of inbound opportunity, and has never really sold itself. Shad offered his three common problem areas, asked why Scott would want the team more sales-minded, and got an answer about process and membership churn rather than a problem. He named it — this does not sound like a priority — and ended the call at fourteen minutes with the door open.

Shad disqualified it himself: 'focusing on sales doesn't sound like it's a top priority right now,' Scott agreed they are 'more passive on it,' and they parted with contact details and nothing scheduled.

Coaching notes❯

The shortest call in the set and arguably the most instructive: a referral with no pain gets fourteen minutes, one 'why,' and a clean no. Nobody gets sent a proposal.

Pattern to copy — best things about it, why did you take the call, three areas with permission, then one 'why do you want that.' Four moves and the qualification is finished.

Fair criticism: he took the first 'it's slow going but it's improved what we do' at face value. There may have been a membership-retention problem underneath, and a second why at 12:50 could have tested whether chasing renewals every year actually costs them anything.

He also never asked about money, timing or who else would be involved — correct here, because there was nothing to qualify, but worth noticing that he stopped the moment the pain failed rather than pushing through the rest of a checklist.
