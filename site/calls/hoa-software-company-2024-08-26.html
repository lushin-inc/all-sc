Fractional COO re-engages Rob to support a new sales manager
Clayton's HOA / volunteer-organization software company (DJ is the incoming fractional COO; '1 to 100 Advisors' in the filename appears to be DJ's own advisory practice)
Rob LimeAdvancedProspect

Rob had previously coached this sales team and ran into an obstructive sales leader, Jeff. DJ has since come in as fractional COO, Jeff is being moved to an advisory role, and Shelley is arriving as the new sales manager ahead of a Series A raise. DJ ran almost the entire call: he briefed Rob on Shelley, the $1-2k deal size, the 20% close rate on heavy inbound, the CRM mess and the new KPIs he is installing. Rob contributed his read from the prior engagement — facilitate decisions faster, stop reading demo enthusiasm as probability to close — asked three questions, and let DJ route the commercial decision back through Clayton.

DJ said he would take it back to Clayton to formalize and that they would touch base monthly, but nothing was scheduled and Rob never proposed a date.

Coaching notes❯

This is not a first conversation. It is a re-engagement call with a new power sponsor on an account Rob previously worked, and it is filed in the FTC folder — treat it as a how-to-handle-a-regime-change call, not a first meeting.

Rob ran no up-front contract and asked roughly four questions in nineteen minutes. The buyer controlled the agenda from 5:32 to the end. It worked because DJ arrived pre-sold, but there is nothing here to study on structuring a call.

No next step was set. 'I'll let Clayton formalize it' and 'us touching base every month or so' is where it ends — no date, no scope conversation, no money. A coach should notice how easily a warm call ends without a calendar entry.

Best coachable material is the diagnostic language at 9:36 to 10:18 and the demo-enthusiasm observation at 12:42 — both are specific enough to reuse with any inbound, low-ticket, committee-buying client.
