central Florida service business owned through Carlos's investment fund

Not a cold first conversation: Carlos and Alejandra have already completed Lushin's FOREST survey and spoken with Shad before, and they open by asking what the investment is. Carlos wants 5 million in revenue at 27 percent GP in three to four years, with 80 percent of current revenue sitting in two accounts, and both leaders say the only thing they are unsure of is sales and specifically their new seller Jennifer. Shad walks their survey answers, proposes the evaluation plus a three-phase structure, and prices it at 4,200 for the evaluation and 2,500 a month plus a 5 percent materials fee. Carlos immediately says he has to line it up against three other coaches.

Priced and in a four-way bake-off; Shad booked a September 3 decision call at 2:30 Eastern with an explicit yes-or-no contract, and owes references with long, complex sales cycles.

Coaching notes❯

The pain question at 17:20 got an honest 'not really', and Shad accepted it and moved on to presenting. Worth debating: should he have kept digging for a compelling reason before pricing a 2,500 a month engagement?

The up-front contract was asked of both people, which surfaced two different buying agendas. Replicate that on any multi-stakeholder first call.

Price was delivered flat, with no pause and no check on reaction, and the bake-off objection arrived 90 seconds later. The recovery, asking how they will compare, is better than the delivery.

The 42:08 close is the thing to steal: a dated decision meeting framed so that a no is welcome and debriefable.
