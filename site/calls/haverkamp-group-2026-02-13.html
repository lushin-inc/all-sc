Talks a willing buyer out of buying, for now
Aaron PrickelStalledProspect

Todd is building a first-ever talent development catalog at a property management company and wants to put OMG on it as a self-select option for leasing agents, after a peer named Chris Lenor showed him a sample. Aaron spends eight minutes establishing who would actually sit with a rep and interpret the findings, and when the answer is property managers who have never seen one, he says plainly that the money would be wasted — $550 a head, "and then you're just going to get mad at me." Todd lands on train-the-trainer, realizes the trainer is a 2026 hire that does not exist yet, and defers the whole thing himself.

Todd concluded "maybe today is not the right time" and will circle back once the trainer role is filled; no date, no follow-up, only Aaron's direct email and an open door.

Coaching notes❯

The whole call is one disqualification done well. Todd arrives ready to buy and Aaron refuses to sell, because the delivery mechanism — property managers interpreting findings in front of a rep — does not exist. That is the reason to listen to this file.

He asks permission before delivering bad news, and Todd actively invites it. Framing the objection as a favor bought him a hearing that a flat warning would not have gotten.

He names a real price unprompted — $550 a head — in the middle of explaining why it would be wasted. Todd notices and says so. A specific number attached to a warning reads as honesty, not as a quote.

"What should I be asking you?" / "Literally nothing" is the moment worth clipping. He declines to answer implementation questions before implementation is possible, on the grounds that they are premature and would have them solving problems that don't exist yet.

He leaves without a next step and without a follow-up date, which is defensible but means re-engagement depends entirely on Todd remembering; a soft calendar hold against the 2026 trainer hire would have cost nothing.
