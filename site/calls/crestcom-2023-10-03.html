Crestcom franchisee pitching Rob on mutual referrals
Rob LimeAdvancedProspect

Doug Peterson owns the Crestcom leadership-training franchise in Indiana and booked this call to propose a referral relationship — he already sends sales-training opportunities to Sandler and wants leadership work coming back the other way. Rob's only real question was what he should listen for in client conversations that ought to make him think of Crestcom, and he used Doug's answer to draw a hard line: anything customer-facing that touches revenue belongs to Lushin, everything else is open. Doug conceded his own three-day sales academy isn't good, Rob called time at fifteen minutes, and they booked coffee at the Lushin office.

Booked an in-person half hour at Lushin's Castleton office for Tuesday the 10th at 8:00 am, where Doug will run his thirty-minute executive overview.

Coaching notes❯

This is not a prospect call. Doug Peterson owns the Crestcom franchise for Indiana, Ohio and Kentucky and is selling Rob on a referral partnership — study it as a call where the Lushin coach is the buyer, and watch how a trained seller behaves on the other side.

'What would I be hearing that should make me think of you?' is the reusable asset here. It converts an abstract partnership into a concrete trigger list, and it works just as well on a client who wants to refer you business.

Rob defines the fence precisely — customer-facing and revenue-impacting is Lushin's, HR/ops/finance leadership is open — which is useful language for any conversation about scope with a partner or a client.

He never asks what Doug wants in return, what volume either side could realistically send, or what a referred Crestcom client actually spends. The partnership stayed abstract, and the second meeting is Doug's thirty-minute overview rather than a working session on referral flow.

No up-front contract at the top; the fifteen-minute slot was pre-agreed but never restated, so Rob had to invoke it mid-sentence at 13:45.
