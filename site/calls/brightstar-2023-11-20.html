Brightstar

Owner says his reps could triple, then hears the price
Brightstar (per the filename; never named in the transcript) — a used-equipment sales business near Middlebury, Indiana, founded 2017, 14 salespeople plus a sales manager
Rob LimeAdvancedProspectTenure at meeting: Pre

Loren had attended Rob's talk and filled out a pre-call questionnaire; Rob opens by sorting the answers for what is both weak and important and lands on prospecting. Loren first says the team is 'generally' fine at getting in front of people, then retracts it, says they are poor at it, and volunteers that output could triple on average — a claim Rob stress-tests from four angles before using it. Rob lays out the team assessment as the first step, prices it at $9,750, offers a $400 single assessment on the sales leader as a reduced-risk entry, then works the decision process and books a follow-up call.

Loren says he can decide in seven to ten days after meeting with his sales manager, and they schedule a phone call for Wednesday the 29th at 3:00 with a calendar invite sent live.

Coaching notes❯

The spine of this call: get the number from the prospect's own mouth (triple), pressure-test it from four angles, then price against it. The $9,750 lands softly because the prospect has already said the gap is worth millions.

'I fired enough bullets into that' at 18:25 is a transition worth memorizing — it tells the buyer why the recommendation is coming now and ties it to his own words.

The $400 leader-only assessment is a clean downsell, but notice it is offered conditionally, after Rob asks the buyer to self-identify as uncertain. Offered up front it would have killed the $9,750.

Rob worked the decision process hard but never asked decision criteria — what would make this a yes versus a no — so the follow-up call starts without knowing what Loren's partner will judge it on.
