Caribbean HR Solutions

Employer of record wants OMG, and Rob prices it live
Rob LimeAdvancedProspect

Debra runs an employer-of-record and recruiting firm placing Caribbean workers with US companies, and met the OMG assessment while filling a sales role for Rob's client Bright Star. She wants to know what it is and whether to adopt it. Rob leads with the contrarian version — recruiters usually dislike OMG because it screens most applicants out — explains sales-specific versus sales-adaptive assessment, walks a live Bright Star registration and candidate report on screen, and then phones his colleague Brian mid-call to work out how a multi-client recruiter license would be priced. He declines her follow-on ask for customer service and leadership assessments.

Debra asked for a quote and Rob owes her pricing for a recruiter-style license once Brian works it out — but no follow-up meeting was scheduled.

Coaching notes❯

The prospect ran more of this call than the coach did — Debra set the agenda twice ('doesn't tell me about your company, though'), asked for price, and asked for the next step. Worth studying as a call where the product demand was already there and process discipline slipped.

No decision-process work: Debra is the CEO, but her HR manager Rushane sat on the whole call unaddressed except for one 'any questions?' at 22:06, and Rob never asked how a tool like this gets adopted internally or what would need to be true to say yes.

The live call to Brian at 22:32 is the standout. Rather than promising to get back to her, he solved his own pricing gap on screen. It cost nothing in credibility and bought her patience for a quote he still owes.

The call ends with no scheduled follow-up — just 'I'll email you.' A single question ('when should we get back together once you have it?') would have made this a clean advance.
