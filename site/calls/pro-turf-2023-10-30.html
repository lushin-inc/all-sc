Owner's son wants training, his leadership team doesn't
Aaron PrickelAdvancedProspectTenure at meeting: Pre

Graham Clarke runs a Toro distributorship across Texas, Oklahoma and Kansas with his father, and has 25 to 30 account executives who are not showing customers an expanded product line — one of his better reps told him flat out he will not cold call. Underneath that sits a management team of former salespeople who won't have hard conversations, and two of eight executives who say they don't have time for training. Aaron spent twenty minutes on why, made Graham name the disqualifier himself, stated the one condition under which the relationship fails, then set homework for Graham and his father ahead of a sixty-minute call with Cody McWhorter.

Aaron will send pre-meeting homework to Graham and Eddie to complete separately, with a 60-minute call booked for Monday the 13th at 1:00 Central including Cody McWhorter.

Coaching notes❯

No up-front contract and no stated agenda. Aaron opened cold with 'what compelled you' and it worked because Graham was ready to talk, but the call ran thirty minutes without a frame or a time box.

19:00 through 19:38 is the best sequence in this batch: make the prospect guess the disqualifier, then state the single condition under which the relationship fails. It is a deal-killer question asked while the prospect is still enthusiastic.

'How much business is out there that you aren't getting?' produced 'easily double' and he left it there. No dollars, no units, no conversion to revenue — and no money of any kind was discussed on a thirty-minute call with a buyer whose father is already inclined to invest.

He surfaced the real obstacle out loud — two of eight leaders won't engage, and the president is himself a micromanager — and still asked for the next step rather than retreating from it. The homework for Graham and Eddie separately is a smart way to test the alignment he just heard is missing.
