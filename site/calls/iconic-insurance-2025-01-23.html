Solo founder hits a ceiling and goes shopping for a mentor
Brian KavickyClosedProspect

Matt was referred by Brad, a client he signed and then asked to coffee, and came in wanting a coach who would tell him what to do rather than ask him what he thinks. He has been stuck at a revenue ceiling for two years, is the only client-facing person in the business, and has hired seven people in two years and let six go. Brian handed him the agenda — 'how would you like to vet me?' — then worked three problems live: the scarcity either/or, the hiring process that has no objectivity in it, and the free-education platform Matt was talked out of two years ago. He priced at $1,500 a month with no contract and an explicit stopping rule, and Matt asked whether he was worthy and told him to have Susie schedule it.

Matt asked 'am I worthy?', Brian said yes, and Matt told him to have Susie reach out to schedule — a verbal yes at $1,500/month with no contract, with the in-person kickoff waiting on his return from Austin and a questionnaire going out first.

Coaching notes❯

Brian spends the first six minutes handing the controls over — what do you want out of this, how would you like to vet me — and never pitches. The prospect sets the agenda and talks himself into the engagement.

The hiring diagnosis is a repeatable sequence: count the failures, separate the one outside his control, name the pattern (subjectivity), prescribe the mechanism (proof before hire), then fix the ad.

He contradicted the prospect twice — 'you should have done this two years ago' and the Marcus Sheridan correction — and both landed because each came with a better mechanism attached, not just a no.

Price arrives at minute 46, after Brian first asks what Matt paid his last coach. The framing ('typically $1,500, but tell me what works and that's where we start') plus zero commitment and an explicit stopping rule is the whole close.

There is no quantified pain anywhere in 53 minutes — no revenue number, no cost of the ceiling, no value on the time he wants back. Brian closed on fit and chemistry, which worked here but leaves nothing to re-anchor on when the invoices start.
