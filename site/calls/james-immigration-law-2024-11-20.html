James Immigration Law

Two people, two agendas, one training check-in
Rob LimeAdvancedProspectTenure at meeting: 1y10m

An existing-client check-in that turns into an agenda mismatch. Rob expects to review the onboarding outline Lushin sent and decide what to add; Yeimira expects Rob to teach her and Daniela the topics so they can deliver training internally to a team with constant turnover. She describes a smart workaround she already ran — making the team watch the videos while she and Daniela were out, then debriefing what they learned and mapping it onto immigration-law examples. Midway through, a second mismatch surfaces: Yeimira thought this meeting was the training. They find the roadmap on screen, pick two topics, and Rob restates the deal for the following Tuesday.

Landed on a concrete agreement for the next Tuesday — Rob trains Yeimira and Daniela on sales for non-salespeople and buyer-seller dynamic, and trains them to teach it.

Worth knowing before you listen❯

Key moments 11❯
7:00
Three questions about the deliverable
“We sent over the standard online outline for onboarding. Is that good? Are you using it? What's the next step for that?”
The right check on an asset you handed a client — used, useful, and what now — asked without softening.
7:37
How the client actually made the content stick
“We made them watch it, and I had a meeting with them the next day after they saw it, and we discussed what they learned and what caught their attention. And I used those things they mentioned with examples of our line of business.”
A client-invented reinforcement loop better than most assignment structures a coach would hand over. Worth stealing.
8:52
Asking the client to spec the next version
“Do you guys have a sense yet of what we should add, or what's next for that? Like, how to build that out more?”
Puts the roadmap in the client's hands — and the answer reveals the mismatch.
9:40
Naming the drift, then backing off it
“I thought the idea was that what we gave you was kind of bare bones and then we would add to it to build it out more. But I mean, it doesn't really necessarily matter to me.”
He surfaces the gap and then immediately deflates it. Listen for how much time that costs over the next five minutes.
10:48
The client corrects the record
“What we were saying is that you were going to teach us on those subjects — remember, like the pipeline, one topic per meeting. And then that way we can take them to them as well, the same way I did with the videos.”
The actual agreement, stated by the client. The payoff of the whole first half.
11:47
Platform was exciting; that wasn't the ask
“The platform was like exciting. But we also wanted us to train ourselves on each topic so we can be more prepared to handle internal sales trainings.”
Distinguishes what the client liked from what the client needed — a distinction worth checking on any content deliverable.
11:57
Parking it and moving to the decision
“Let me ask you this, if you don't mind, and then we could come back to that. We have the training on the calendar for next Tuesday — do you know what you want to train on next week?”
The recovery move: stop relitigating the last meeting and get the next one specified.
14:45
The second mismatch surfaces
“No, I thought today was part of the training. I thought we were going to maybe start today with the training.”
Twelve minutes in, it turns out she expected to be trained right now. An expensive illustration of what a one-line agenda confirmation would have prevented.
15:16
The missing course
“I don't see the James Immigration Law training anymore in the platform.”
Three minutes of dead air hunting for content inside the LMS — a real friction point worth knowing about before a session.
17:46
Why she wants to be trained to train
“We want to train ourselves on those topics so we can provide training for future employees. We get people constantly moving. They joined the meeting and some of them have never seen you before, and we didn't have tracking of what was done.”
The underlying business reason, which finally explains everything she has been asking for since minute seven.
18:30
Restating the deal
“So next Tuesday, you and Daniela and I — this will be me training the topics, and then also training you guys to teach the topics.”
Closes the loop by repeating the agreement back for confirmation, which is the one thing that was missing from the previous meeting.

Coaching notes❯

Mislabeled as a prospect conversation — this is an existing-client working session with no selling in it, which is why it carries only next-step and ufc-next milestones.

The whole call is a case study in what a thirty-second up-front contract prevents. Two separate expectation gaps surface — who builds the content, and whether today is the training — and both were created by not confirming the agenda at the start of this call or the end of the last one.

Rob's recovery at 11:57 is the thing to copy: he stops arguing about what was agreed, parks it, and spends the remaining time specifying the next meeting instead.

Yeimira's real requirement — train the trainers, because turnover means the same session gets repeated forever — only emerges at 17:46. A question about why she wants it that way, asked at minute nine, would have saved the call.

Transcript quality is fine but English is a second language for both client speakers and several lines are fragmentary; the training platform is referred to once as 'this handler platform' (Sandler) and once as 'the insandler.'
