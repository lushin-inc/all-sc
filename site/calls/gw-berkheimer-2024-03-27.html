HR referral with no urgency behind it
Shad TidlerStalledProspect

Alex Perry had delivered a presentation-skills class to GW Berkheimer's management team and handed Shad to Maureen, the HR leader, after the marketing director asked about customer-service and sales training. Shad gathered the background, asked what was driving it now, and proposed a three-way call with the marketing director. Maureen then revealed the real state of play: the marketing director never followed up with Alex, the department is consumed with an April sales meeting and three new locations opening April 30, and the project is nowhere near the top of his list. Shad agreed to send website links and pinned a May 15 phone call to find out whether a meeting is worth setting.

No meeting with the actual buyer; Shad sent literature and booked a May 15 check-in call with an explicit out.

Coaching notes❯

He pinned a date and time rather than accepting 'let's touch base in May,' and gave her an explicit out on the call. That combination is why a low-urgency opportunity still has a pulse.

The literature request went unexamined. No question about what the marketing director would need to see, or what would have to be true for this to reach his top three.

Nothing was quantified on this call: no revenue, no headcount, no growth number, no cost of doing nothing. A single 'what should that number be' would have given the May 15 call something to anchor on.

Asking 'what's driving this now?' is what produced the April sales meeting, the three acquisitions and the honest statement about urgency. Without it he would have walked away thinking this was warm.
