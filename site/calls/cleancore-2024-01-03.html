CleanCore

Chemical-free disruptor rebuilding its rep channel
chemical-free; aqueous ozone cleaning technology, roughly $3M in revenue, selling into Jan-San distribution, restaurants, schools and hotels
Brian KavickyAdvancedSales1on1

Matt bought CleanCore a year ago and cannot break into an industry locked up by Ecolab and P&G, despite a product that is cheaper, safer and wins demos; he and his CRO Gary want to go from $3M to $30M. Brian spends almost the whole call diagnosing and teaching — the gain/pain scale, why 'too good to be true' is the real objection, the dummy curve and training new reps with less product knowledge, owner-rep versus distribution, why the lease should be a binary rather than a good-better-best, and the trade to get the hotel chain to reveal what it pays today. Matt asks for pricing unprompted at 41:01, hears three grand a month with no contract, and says he'll green-light it with Michael.

Matt says he'll connect with Michael, asks whether Brian can start as early as next week, and Brian describes how they'd put defined working time on the calendar — but no date is actually set.

The dummy curve
“On your very first day in selling you're at your optimal. Your first day is your best day. Over time, as your experience gets better, you start making assumptions and you start using talk tracks, and you stop asking questions.”
The teaching concept that reframes 'green reps are a risk' into 'green reps are the advantage.'
20:49
Train them less, not more
“Don't train them about the product or the technology or anything beyond what they minimally need to know. The less they know about how it works, why it works, the more effective they will actually be, because what they're selling against is not using chemicals. They're not selling a technology. They're selling against change of chemicals.”
Counterintuitive and specific; it's also the moment Matt starts taking notes out loud.
21:51
The doctor analogy
“If you went into your doctor and the doctor said, let me tell you how good I am, let me tell you all the chemistry I know, you'd be like, oh my gosh. Instead they go, what's wrong? They have to be so good at diagnosis that you go, they diagnosed me right, I'll take the pill. I don't even have to know what the pill is.”
The cleanest version of the diagnose-don't-pitch argument in this batch.
23:18
The opening question for a green rep
“The question they should be asking is: how come you guys don't use our equipment? And the first thing they're going to say is, well, we don't know what your equipment is. Oh, I can get into that. But why do you use Ecolab?”
A script, not a concept — and it's the Columbo posture made concrete.
35:24
Binary, not good-better-best
“People make decisions binary. If I said Long John Silver's, Burger King or McDonald's today, you eliminated one to come up with a choice. Your brain, as soon as you see good, better, best, is to eliminate one.”
Reframes the lease-versus-buy question from a pricing problem into a choice-architecture problem.
40:26
Trade price for cost
“I'm going to give you our price, but I need to know you're going to give me your cost, because we're going to use that to create rules of thumb for future prices on people who aren't you. You're getting locked in. Other people are getting a higher price.”
Turns 'let us see your pricing first' from a loss into an exchange, with a benefit the hotel actually wants.

The engagement in one sentence
“The relationship is, hey, we're going to pay a monthly amount and we're going to exploit the crap out of you.”
No proposal, no deck, no scope document — and the prospect says 'got it' and moves to price.
