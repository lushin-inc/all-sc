A Plus Staffing

Onboarding kickoff with two new reps, not a prospect
Shad TidlerClosedProspectTenure at meeting: 1y4m

This is not a first-time prospect conversation — it is a program kickoff with two brand-new salespeople at an existing client, A Plus Staffing, whose leaders Brittany and Allison had already set it up. Shad opened with a full agenda, walked them through the Sales Essentials weekly classes, the twice-monthly A Plus-only coaching sessions, the newsletter, and Sandler Online, then spent the back third asking what each of them wanted worked on. Anthony named adult-to-adult positioning and reversing on the phone; Kelsey named setting role expectations and converting referral promises into actual names. He mapped each request to a specific tool and closed by recapping who would send what.

Already-sold engagement; the call ends with Shad listing the setup steps — Brian Daniels sends class access and Sandler Online logins, Shweta sends the recurring coaching invites.

0:07
A full agenda before anyone speaks
“I know we've got an hour, but I think we're going to need maybe 30 minutes. So here's what I plan to cover with you.”
Gives back half the booked time before asking for anything — a small move that sets the tone for every session after it.
10:12
Why a rep leaves after a $300K year
“Ended up billing around 300k in my first year. But this wasn't really the type of company feel I was looking for, and I felt the leadership was just very young, to be transparent. I didn't really feel like I was getting the mentoring that would foster long-term success.”
The prospect's own words on why coaching and mentorship beat comp for a strong young rep — useful language for any hiring conversation.
13:53
Not a script, your voice
“How do I make this natural to me, that I can use day to day, that sounds like me and not like a robot and not like 'here's how Shad said I've got to say it.' We don't believe in a lot of scripts. We believe in giving you processes, tools, guidelines, rules, and then you putting it into your own voice.”
Pre-empts the single biggest objection new reps have to sales methodology, in the first session.
18:00
Training wheels
“When you took the training wheels off, what happened the first time? You wiped out. You skinned up your knees and elbows, you had to adjust and make tweaks for the next time.”
Sets the expectation that the tools will fail on first use, which is the thing that otherwise makes new reps quietly stop using them.
18:45
Pre-authorizes the mid-cycle call for help
“I want you to know that when you hit those things and it's not working perfect, that frustration — that's normal. In between sessions, if you're like, I tried that, it didn't work, I need some help — reach out. Do not be bashful about reaching out in between.”
Names the exact feeling and the exact behavior he wants, so the ask later feels like compliance rather than weakness.
19:54
Lets them set the curriculum
“What are some things that you're hoping that we work on — whether it's past challenges selling that you ran into, or if it's 'I want to figure this out'?”
Ten minutes of the kickoff spent collecting their agenda, which he writes down and references in the recap.
21:03
Maps a vague want onto two named tools
“There'll be that establishing expectations — that tool we call an up-front contract. Part of it also is we'll work through something called transactional analysis, which is how do we stay in that adult-to-adult, especially when they're in conflict or putting pressure on us.”
Anthony says 'adult to adult' and gets two concrete tools back in fifteen seconds — proof the program already has an answer.
23:17
Why referrals stall, and what to do instead
“They're not giving referrals every day, and they don't necessarily know what makes a good referral for you. I went through his LinkedIn and sent him a specific list of folks and said, here's some that I think may fit. Out of that he sent me four good referrals last week.”
Answers Kelsey's 'they just say we'll think about it' with a mechanic that is immediately usable, plus his own live example.
24:56
The new-car analogy for referral awareness
“If you bought a new car and suddenly you see those cars all over the road — well, they were always there. You're just training your brain to look for them. When you're asking for referrals, people have connections. We've got to help turn that part of their brain on.”
Explains why specificity works, so the tactic survives the first time it does not produce names.

Coaching notes❯

Filename problem: this is labeled Prospect / FTC but it is a kickoff with two new hires at an existing A Plus Staffing engagement. Anyone studying it for first-conversation technique will be studying the wrong thing — it is a model onboarding session instead.

The one transferable first-conversation element is the opening: a full agenda, time given back, and 'anything else either of you want to cover' before anyone has spoken.

Roughly six minutes (3:42 to 10:05) are consumed by Anthony's microphone failing and a dial-in workaround. Shad keeps the session moving by giving Kelsey the class overview while Anthony reconnects — a good recovery, but it leaves Anthony with no program overview of his own.

The back half is the strongest coaching: he collects what each rep wants, names the specific tool for each request, and recaps both the logistics and their requests at the close.

Lushin appears as 'Lucian'/'Lucien' and Shad as 'Chad'/'Shad' throughout the transcript.
