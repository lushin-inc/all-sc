Operation Hired

Founder wants to hire a closer; coach sells infrastructure first
veteran-owned staffing and recruiting
Shad TidlerAdvancedProspect

Matt called about one thing: he needs a B2B salesperson and doesn't know where to look. His new staffing business serves two customers — veterans and spouses on the B2C side, which is working, and employers on the B2B side, where he gets first meetings and never gets the contract. Shad refused to answer the hiring question and instead asked whether the bird cage was ready for the bird: could he show a new rep a documented sales process that he knows works. Matt said no. Shad walked him through what's missing, absorbed the real objection — Matt doesn't have the time to build it — and countered by asking how he would ever know whether a hire was doing the right things. Matt conceded, and a working session was booked.

Booked Tuesday the 10th at 8:00 a.m. for 45 minutes with a ten-minute four-S survey sent ahead, and an explicit out at the end of that meeting if Matt wants to run with it himself.

Coaching notes❯

This is the clearest reframe in the batch: a founder asks for help hiring a closer and leaves with a working session on sales infrastructure, without ever being told his question was wrong. The pivot runs 6:54 to 8:27 and is worth listening to end to end.

The time objection at 15:04 is handled by changing what Matt is buying — speed and a way to evaluate a future hire — rather than by debating whether he has time. That's the move most coaches miss.

Money never comes up. There is no indication of what Matt could spend, what he has spent on recruiting, or what a salesperson would cost him, which would have strengthened the argument that building the structure first is cheaper than a bad hire.

Shad also never asks who else decides, though Matt says 'we' throughout and clearly has at least one partner. Worth flagging as the gap to close in the follow-up.
