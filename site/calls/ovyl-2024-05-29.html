Founder scaling past founder-led sales, no next step
Rob LimeStalledProspect

Evan runs a 17-person product design and engineering agency where his partner is the only salesperson, and deal flow is the bottleneck. Rob spent 20 straight minutes qualifying — a thermometer question on how much help they need, how hard the thing is to sell, what the average deal is worth, what the rep would have to close, and what they would pay someone that rare — before asking permission to say anything about himself. He talked Evan out of part-time or split-role selling, explained that he only starts with hiring if it is part of a longer build, and framed money as something a CEO accepts easily when the problem work was done first. The call ended with someone knocking on Evan's door and no next step agreed.

Evan said it was 'mostly exploratory' and that he needed to sync with his leadership team; Rob's 'what do you want to do next' never got answered because Evan was interrupted and left it at 'we can follow up on email.'

Coaching notes❯

The qualification sequence is the lesson here: readiness, difficulty of the sale, deal size, required quota, comp, locality, who manages, and how good they are at developing people — all before he says one word about Lushin.

He disqualifies two of the prospect's own ideas (part-time selling, hire-only projects) and gains ground doing it. Disagreeing well is the whole move.

The close is the weak point. He asks 'what do you want to do next' once, Evan gets interrupted by someone at his door, and Rob accepts 'we can follow up on email' without proposing a date or a reason to meet again. A 34-minute call this good deserved a booked next step.

No budget was ever discussed for Lushin itself — only what a salesperson would cost. Evan left without knowing what working with Rob costs, which makes the internal leadership conversation harder to win.
