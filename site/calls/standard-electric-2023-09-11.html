Standard Electric

Director asks for onboarding class, gets assessment first
Aaron PrickelStalledProspectTenure at meeting: Pre

Tammy wants a day of sales training she can drop into new-hire orientation for about ten people who carry a sales title but have never actually sold. Aaron reframed the structure (smaller doses over longer time), established that none of them know what they don't know and that the company collects no sales data at hire, then asked permission to suggest the assessment step and priced it on the spot at $250 a head plus $2,500 for the overview. She agreed on approach, asked for something she could circulate, and the call ended abruptly with her asking to call him back.

She asked him to build a short custom video she can share with the other three directors, then cut the call short — 'Could I call you right back?' — with no follow-up meeting scheduled.

Coaching notes❯

He converted a request for a one-day onboarding class into an assessment-first conversation without ever telling her no. The reframe at 2:35 is the whole move.

Price came out at minute eleven of a seventeen-minute call, unprompted and unhedged, right after she agreed on the approach — not at the end, not in a follow-up email.

No next meeting was booked. The call ends on 'Could I call you right back?' with nothing on the calendar and a video he owes her. That is the one clear miss, and it is a good example of how a warm call can still stall.

The decision-process questions at 15:18 and 16:27 are worth copying verbatim: who's at the table, does everyone bring their own vendor, and do the others see it the way you do.

Transcript quality is poor on names: Fireflies renders Tammy as Cammy, Timmy and Tim, and Aaron as 'Erin.' The header date (November 21, 2023) also disagrees with the filename date.
